# Candidates — A Day of One-Line Pitches (v1)

Judge's criteria, in order: (1) persuasiveness — a concrete reason to say
yes now, handing the idea over feels easy and low risk; (2) clarity — one
read is enough, plain words; (3) truth — nothing overpromised about what an
AI agent can do with a first app idea on make.fun; (4) warmth and craft —
sounds like a person talking to a friend, fresh turn of phrase, not a
template or slogan; (5) surprise at the end + an everyday truth the friend
recognises. Each criterion scored /5, total /25. All candidates are one
sentence, 180–240 characters, mention make.fun and the first app idea, and
carry a call to action.

## A — SELECTED (220 characters, 22/25)

> You have had that app idea in your head for months, so just describe it to an AI agent on make.fun and let it build your first app while you watch — the hardest part was never the code, it was finally saying it out loud.

- Persuasiveness 4/5 — "just describe it" + "while you watch" makes handing
  the idea over feel easy and low risk; "in your head for months" is the
  concrete reason to act now.
- Clarity 4/5 — plain words, one read, no jargon.
- Truth 5/5 — promises only that the agent builds the first app from a
  description; nothing about speed, cost or quality is claimed.
- Warmth and craft 5/5 — sounds like a friend, not a slogan.
- Surprise + everyday truth 4/5 — the twist lands at the very end and names
  a truth every beginner recognises: saying the idea out loud was the real
  barrier, not the code.

## B (211 characters, 20/25)

> Tell an AI agent on make.fun the app idea you keep bringing up at dinner, and it will build your first app for you — you only have to describe it, and honestly, describing it is the part you already do for free.

- Persuasiveness 4/5, Clarity 4/5, Truth 4/5, Warmth and craft 4/5,
  Surprise + everyday truth 4/5. "Bringing up at dinner" is a warm everyday
  truth, but the ending ("the part you already do for free") is a weaker
  twist than A's and "for free" invites a pricing read the pitch does not
  need.

## C (210 characters, 20/25)

> You don't need to learn to code to finally build the app idea you've been talking about for years — just tell it to an AI agent on make.fun, and your first app will be waiting by the time your coffee gets cold.

- Persuasiveness 4/5, Clarity 5/5, Truth 3/5, Warmth and craft 4/5,
  Surprise + everyday truth 4/5. The clearest of the six, but "waiting by
  the time your coffee gets cold" overpromises build speed and risks the
  truth criterion against a judge that checks what holds up.

## D (205 characters, 22/25)

> Give the app idea you've been carrying around in your head to an AI agent on make.fun and watch it become your first real app — no coding, no course, no waiting for someday, just the idea you already have.

- Persuasiveness 4/5, Clarity 5/5, Truth 5/5, Warmth and craft 4/5,
  Surprise + everyday truth 4/5. Tied with A on total, but the ending is a
  list ("no coding, no course, no waiting for someday") that reads closer to
  a template, while A's closing twist is fresher and more memorable. Loses
  the tie-break on freshness.

## E (201 characters, 20/25)

> That app idea you bring up every time we talk, just describe it to an AI agent on make.fun and let it build your first app — you bring the idea, it does the coding, and you finally stop saying one day.

- Persuasiveness 4/5, Clarity 4/5, Truth 5/5, Warmth and craft 4/5,
  Surprise + everyday truth 3/5. "You finally stop saying one day" is a
  good everyday truth, but the opening comma-splice ("...we talk, just
  describe it...") is the clumsiest grammar of the six and the middle
  ("you bring the idea, it does the coding") is a plain split of labour
  rather than a twist.

## F (226 characters, 21/25)

> Stop keeping your app idea in your notes app and hand it to an AI agent on make.fun instead — it builds your first app from a plain description, and the only skill you need is the one you already have: talking about your idea.

- Persuasiveness 4/5, Clarity 4/5, Truth 5/5, Warmth and craft 4/5,
  Surprise + everyday truth 4/5. Strong and warm, but "the only skill you
  need is the one you already have" lands as reassurance rather than
  surprise, and at 226 characters it is the longest candidate for no extra
  persuasion.

## Why A wins

A and D tie at 22/25. A wins the tie-break: its ending ("the hardest part
was never the code, it was finally saying it out loud") is the freshest turn
of phrase in the set and the most memorable single line a judge reads once —
it reframes the friend's real obstacle instead of listing what is not
needed. A's opening ("in your head for months") is also the most concrete
hook: it names the friend's own situation before asking for anything.
